### Where agent identity standards are heading

- **The market now calls this "agent identity" / "non-human identity (NHI) management,"** distinct from service-account/API-key management. Gartner projects 40% of enterprise apps will ship with embedded agents by end of 2026 (up from <5% in 2025), which is driving urgency [MS Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-entra-blog/govern-ai-agent-identities-and-access-the-same-way-you-govern-your-employees/4529302).
- **Microsoft has the most mature dedicated primitive**: Entra Agent ID extends the full Zero Trust stack (Conditional Access, Identity Protection, PIM) to agents specifically, reaching GA in April 2026 — this is the closest thing to an industry reference architecture right now.
- **AWS's answer is credential/token-centric** rather than a directory-object model: AgentCore Identity + OBO token exchange solves "propagate the human's identity through an agent chain without sharing credentials," which is a narrower but production-relevant slice of the same problem.
- **A dedicated NHI vendor market has emerged** (Astrix Security, Oasis Security, Entro Security, Token Security, Aembit, Clutch, GitGuardian) extending from secrets/service-account management into agent-specific discovery and policy. Consolidation is already underway: Cisco announced intent to acquire Astrix for ~$400M (May 4, 2026); Astrix ended standalone new-license sales June 30, 2026. Oasis raised $120M from Craft Ventures for "identity governance for machines, services, and AI agents." NHI access management market: ~$11.3B (2025) → ~$12.2B (2026) [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-cisco-astrix-ai-agent-identity-market-cons/), [Oasis](https://www.oasis.security/).
- **No universal open standard yet.** An academic survey (arXiv 2604.23280, "AI Identity: Standards, Gaps, and Research Directions for AI Agents") frames this as an active research gap rather than solved problem [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23280).
- **Direction**: expect agent identity to converge on (1) a directory-object model (Microsoft's bet), (2) delegated/OBO token propagation for tool-calling chains (AWS's bet), and (3) a discovery+policy layer bolted onto existing IAM/PAM stacks (the NHI vendor bet) — consolidating via M&A into the big three cloud IAM stacks within 12-18 months.

**Tamper-evident/signed logs — does anyone do it?** Among the *mainstream enterprise platforms* researched (Claude, Copilot, Cursor, Devin, Bedrock, Gemini Enterprise, Agentforce), **none publicly document cryptographically hash-chained or signed audit logs** — they describe "audit logs," "compliance APIs," and SIEM-exportable streams, but not tamper-evidence as a designed property. Tamper-evident/hash-chained logging is currently a **niche/emerging-vendor feature**, not a mainstream enterprise-platform one: purpose-built tools like Hashline, Trinitite, DeepInspect, and OSS projects like halo-record and OpenFang's Merkle Hash-Chain Audit Trail are explicitly built around this, often citing EU AI Act Article 12, Fannie Mae LL-2026-04, HIPAA, DORA, and NIST AI RMF as the compliance drivers [DeepInspect](https://www.deepinspect.ai/blog/tamper-evident-audit-logs-for-ai), [Hashline](https://hashline.dev/). **This is a clear differentiator opportunity for Arc's `SignedChainSink`.**

**EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF**: vendors reference these as design targets (Salesforce ties Trust Layer messaging to them implicitly; niche audit-log vendors cite EU AI Act Article 12 explicitly) but no major platform in this set claims formal EU AI Act conformity assessment completion — the Act's high-risk-system obligations are still being operationalized industry-wide as of mid-2026.

## (d) FinOps & Observability

- **OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions**: a real, converging standard (`gen_ai.*` span/attribute vocabulary) covering LLM client spans, agent spans, tool spans, and metrics (token usage, latency) as of spec v1.41. Explicitly still **pre-stable/experimental — no 1.0 release**, names can still change [Greptime](https://greptime.com/blogs/2026-05-09-opentelemetry-genai-semantic-conventions), [techbytes cheat sheet](https://techbytes.app/posts/opentelemetry-genai-agent-semconv-cheat-sheet-2026/). This is the vendor-neutral schema most observability tooling (Datadog, Langfuse, LangSmith, Braintrust) is converging on.
- **Cost crisis driving the FinOps push**: Claude Code reportedly hit 84% penetration across Uber's ~5,000-engineer org by early 2026, and Uber's entire 2026 AI budget was reportedly exhausted in four months; 78% of IT leaders reported unexpected charges from consumption-based AI pricing in 2026 [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ai-agent-cloud-costs/), [Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/ai-finops-2026-73-percent-blow-budget-cfo-fix).
- **Claude Enterprise spend controls** (launched July 2, 2026): model-level entitlements, spend-threshold alerts, per-user/org caps; Analytics API integrates with Datadog Cloud Cost Management, CloudZero, and Finout for chargeback [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319687/20260704/claude-enterprise-spend-controls-arrive-agentic-ai-bills-blow-past-budgets.htm).
- **Google Cloud** shipped "Spend Caps" + AI cost visibility (Next '26) as a dedicated cost-governance product surface [Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/cost-management/introducing-spend-caps-ai-cost-visibility-next26).
- **Microsoft Copilot Studio**: per-environment Copilot-credit tracking with spend caps to prevent uncontrolled agent-usage cost exposure [PowerTricks governance ref](https://www.powertricks.io/copilot-studio-governance/).
- **Common pattern**: hard workflow/agent-level spend limits that auto-pause execution on breach, plus usage APIs designed to feed third-party FinOps tools rather than each vendor building its own chargeback UI.

## (e) Procurement Reality

- **Pricing models cluster around**: (1) per-seat + usage hybrid (Claude Enterprise: $20/seat + API-rate usage; Cursor: $40/user Team → negotiated Enterprise; Microsoft Agent 365: $15/user/mo standalone or bundled at $99 in M365 E7), or (2) fully negotiated ACV (Sourcegraph: $15k small-team to $250k+ enterprise; Glean: undisclosed, "triangulated from marketplace listings and RFP gossip").
- **Minimum commitments**: Claude Enterprise self-serve requires 20-seat minimum before a sales call is even needed.
- **The Microsoft-agent-tier pricing template ($30 add-on to M365, as with Copilot)** is described as the pattern the rest of the industry is copying for "agent tier" upsells — expect 30-50% price uplift over search/assist-only tiers [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/glean).
- **What a CISO asks for in review** (2026 checklists converge on): SOC 2 report, data residency/isolation guarantees, zero-retention/no-training-on-customer-data commitments, FedRAMP status (or explicit non-pursuit), threat model / TEE isolation claims, cryptographic attestation, identity-inheritance mapping (what does the agent act *as*?), and — increasingly — an inventory question: "what shadow AI/agents already exist in our environment that we don't know about?" [Zenity governance checklist](https://zenity.io/blog/security/ai-agent-governance), [Docket procurement checklist](https://www.docket.io/blog/the-2026-ai-procurement-checklist-vetting-your-ai-agent-for-security-privacy-and-trust).
- **A tool that fails the security review does not get a second chance on better UX or pricing** — procurement gates *before* other strengths matter, per CTO-level guidance aimed at coding agents specifically [Augment Code](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/cto-ai-coding-checklist).
- **Common adoption blockers**: consumption-based billing unpredictability (see FinOps section), missing model-provenance documentation (a hard GSAR 552.239-7001 disqualifier if adopted), closed architecture with no source-level auditability and no public FedRAMP roadmap, and unresolved non-human-identity inventory gaps.

## (f) THE UNMET NEEDS LIST — Ranked, With Evidence

1. **No mainstream platform combines FedRAMP High + tamper-evident/signed audit logs + true air-gap self-host in one product.** Vendors split into "federal-authorized but not tamper-evident-logged" (Palantir, Ask Sage, Salesforce Agentforce, Second Front-brokered vendors) vs. "tamper-evident-logged but not federally authorized" (Hashline, Trinitite, DeepInspect, OpenFang — all niche/early-stage). This is the exact intersection Arc's Four Pillars (Identity/Sign/Authorize/Audit with `SignedChainSink`) target, and evidence across ~15 vendors surveyed shows nobody else is there yet. [Multiple sources above; synthesis]

2. **Agent identity is fragmenting into three incompatible bets with no interoperability standard.** Microsoft (directory-object model via Entra Agent ID), AWS (delegated OBO token propagation via AgentCore Identity), and a third-party NHI vendor layer (Astrix/Oasis, now consolidating via M&A into Cisco and others) are each solving a slightly different slice, with an arXiv survey (2604.23280) explicitly calling this an open research gap rather than a settled standard. Buyers building multi-cloud/multi-vendor agent fleets have no portable agent-identity credential today. [Microsoft, AWS, CSA, arXiv sources above]

3. **Cursor's admitted gap — no reconstructable AI-activity audit trail (only admin-action logs) — is likely industry-wide, not Cursor-specific**, since no other coding-agent vendor in this survey volunteered a counter-claim of full prompt/tool-call audit reconstruction. This directly blocks incident forensics ("what did the agent actually read/do") which SOC 2/ISO audits increasingly ask for. [vibe-eval source]

4. **FedRAMP timelines (12-18 months from sponsorship letter to ATO) are structurally too slow for agentic AI's release cadence.** The 20x pilot program (2-month target) exists precisely because the GSA/FedRAMP Board recognizes the mismatch, but it's narrowly scoped to "conversational AI for routine repeated use," not general agentic/tool-calling systems — leaving a gap for anything more autonomous than a chatbot. [GSA 20x announcement, FirmAdapt]

5. **Model-provenance and supply-chain documentation is becoming a hard procurement disqualifier (GSAR 552.239-7001)**, and most vendors in this survey do not publish SBOM-equivalent provenance for their agent/tool supply chain in the way software supply-chain security now expects. [GAO-cited gap analysis]

6. **Cost predictability remains unsolved at the agent-autonomy layer.** Spend caps and workflow-level pause-on-breach are new (mid-2026) reactive fixes to a crisis (Uber's 4-month budget burn) rather than a designed-in constraint; none of the surveyed platforms described *proactive* per-task cost forecasting, only reactive circuit breakers. [TechRepublic, Beri.net, TechTimes]

7. **"Borrowed accreditation boundary" (FedStart, Game Warden) is becoming the default federal go-to-market path rather than vendors achieving independent ATOs** — functional today, but it means the federal buyer's actual security posture is only as strong as the boundary owner, and vendors inside these boundaries (e.g., Cohere via Second Front) have not independently demonstrated the Four-Pillar depth Arc claims. This is a market-entry pattern worth exploiting: an Arc federal offering with independent FedRAMP-track design from day one is differentiated versus "renting" Palantir's or Second Front's boundary. [Second Front, Palantir sources]

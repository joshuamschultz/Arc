# Competitive read — 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30

## What actually changed

One thing, and it is not the thing that got attention. Two competitors published usage-based
pricing pages this quarter, which generated most of the internal noise. The change that
matters is quieter: enterprise buyers in our own lost deals began asking for SOC 2 Type II
as a gate rather than a preference. That is a condition, not a move, and it is the only item
here that presses on a placed bet.

## Moves

| Actor | Action | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northwind | Published usage-based pricing page | 2026-05-12 | Their pricing page, captured |
| Meridian | Announced "enterprise tier" in a blog post | 2026-06-03 | Company blog — **announced, not shipped** |
| Meridian | Hired 4 enterprise AEs (LinkedIn headcount) | 2026-04 to 2026-06 | LinkedIn, counted directly |

Meridian's enterprise tier is announced only. Their docs, trial flow, and contract templates
show no enterprise SKU as of 2026-06-30. The AE hiring is the real signal; the blog post is
the advertisement of an intention.

## Conditions

- **SOC 2 Type II moved from preference to gate.** Three of our four enterprise losses this
  quarter named it explicitly in writing (deal cards `[[acme]]`, `[[globex]]`, `[[initech]]`).
  Two different buyers used near-identical procurement language, which suggests a shared
  compliance checklist rather than three independent opinions.
- **Usage-based pricing is becoming table stakes in the mid-market.** Two of three named
  competitors now publish it. One more and this stops being a trend and becomes the default
  buyer expectation.

## Noise (and why)

- **Northwind's pricing page redesign.** Dismissed. The rate card is materially unchanged;
  the page is a repackaging. Reacting to presentation as if it were pricing strategy is how
  a quarter gets spent on nothing.
- **"Everyone is moving upmarket" (internal Slack, 2026-06).** Dismissed as unsourced. It
  traces to one person reading the Meridian blog post. One announcement is not a market.
- **Meridian's funding round (2026-05).** Dismissed for this read. Capital enables moves; it
  is not itself a move. Revisit if hiring or shipping changes.

## Bets under pressure

- `[[move-upmarket]]` — **pressed hard** by the SOC 2 condition. The bet is not contradicted,
  but its sequencing is: the assumption `[[enterprise-close-without-soc2]]` is now challenged
  on the evidence above.
- `[[self-serve-expansion]]` — **lightly pressed** by usage-based pricing becoming standard.
  Not a threat yet; it changes what our pricing page has to answer, not what we are building.
- `[[land-and-expand-mid-market]]` — **no bet touched.** Nothing this quarter bears on it.

## Recommendation

**No competitive response.** Nothing here calls for reacting to Meridian or Northwind. The
one action worth taking is internal and already known: close SOC 2 Type II. That was true
before this read; the read confirms the priority rather than changing it.

## What would change our mind

- Meridian's enterprise SKU appears in their **docs or contract templates** — announced
  becomes shipped, and the AE hiring gets a product behind it.
- A **third** competitor publishes usage-based pricing — trend becomes buyer default, and
  our pricing page has to answer for it.
- An enterprise deal **closes without** SOC 2 Type II — the condition above is weaker than
  three losses suggested, and `[[enterprise-close-without-soc2]]` goes back to holding.

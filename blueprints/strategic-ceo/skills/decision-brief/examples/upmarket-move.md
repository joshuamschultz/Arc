# Decision — Move upmarket to enterprise, or stay mid-market

## The choice

- **A — Move upmarket.** Reposition on enterprise deals ($150k+ ACV), hire two enterprise
  AEs and a solutions engineer, rebuild security/compliance collateral.
- **B — Stay mid-market.** Keep the current motion, spend the same money on self-serve
  conversion and expansion.

Not an option: "do both." Cards `[[move-upmarket]]` and `[[self-serve-expansion]]` both
claim the same two hires in Q3.

## What would have to be true

**For A (move upmarket):**
- Mid-market ACV growth is genuinely capped, not just slow this quarter. `unknown` — we
  have two soft quarters, which is not enough to call a ceiling.
- We can close an enterprise deal without SOC 2 Type II. Contradicted: three of the four
  losses in `[[signals]]` named the missing audit explicitly.
- An enterprise AE ramps inside two quarters. `unknown` — no prior enterprise hire to compare.

**For B (stay mid-market):**
- Self-serve conversion responds to spend. Holding — the last two experiments moved
  conversion 1.4pp on a small budget (`[[self-serve-conversion]]`).
- The current motion still has room. Holding, weakly — see the ACV question above.

## The case against the recommendation

The strongest case against staying mid-market: we may be reading a structural ceiling as a
temporary dip. If mid-market ACV really is capped, then every quarter spent optimizing
self-serve is a quarter of compounding enterprise pipeline we never started, and enterprise
pipeline has a 9-month lag. Waiting two quarters to "get more data" is not free — it is
choosing to arrive 9 months later. That is a real cost and it is the reason this decision is
uncomfortable rather than obvious.

Also against: the SOC 2 blocker is fixable on a known timeline. Treating a solvable
compliance gap as a reason not to move upmarket confuses a sequencing problem with a
strategy problem.

## Reversibility

**Mostly reversible, with one irreversible component.** The positioning, collateral, and
pricing experiments can be unwound in a quarter. The two enterprise AE hires cannot — that is
roughly $400k committed and a year of ramp before we learn anything. Reversible parts should
move fast; the hires should not move until the falsification test below has run.

## Recommendation

**B, with a scoped enterprise probe — do not hire yet.**

Run four enterprise deals through the existing team while closing SOC 2 Type II. Keep the
self-serve spend. Decide on the AE hires when the probe reports.

## What we accept

We accept arriving in enterprise roughly two quarters later than option A, and we accept
that the existing AEs will be stretched across two motions and will resent it. We are buying
the answer to "is mid-market actually capped" for the price of that delay, rather than paying
$400k for it in hiring.

## Revisit when

- SOC 2 Type II is issued (expected Q3), **or**
- the enterprise probe closes its 4th deal, **or**
- mid-market ACV posts a third consecutive soft quarter

whichever comes first. Assumption `[[midmarket-acv-capped]]` is the one to watch; its
falsification test is a third soft quarter.
